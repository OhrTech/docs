---
title: Permissions and limits
description: Grant each integration only what it needs, on only the devices it works with, from only the networks it runs on.
---

Access is per organisation and checked on every call, never cached in the token: change a
permission in the OhrTech Portal and the very next call follows it.

## Permissions

| Permission | Lets the caller | People | Service accounts |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ViewDevices` | See devices and fleets: status, installed apps, enrolments, tasks, sessions. Ask a device to check in. | Every member | When granted |
| `ViewSettings` | See devices' managed settings and what the devices applied | Every member | When granted |
| `EditSettings` | Change devices' settings, push and re-apply them | Every member | When granted |
| `MoveDevices` | Move devices between fleets | Every member | When granted |
| `SendMessages` | Show messages on devices' screens | Every member | When granted |
| `ManageApps` | Add apps, upload versions, hold, offer or enforce them, assign apps to fleets | Every member | When granted |
| `RemoteControl` | Open, join and end remote control sessions | When granted | When granted |
| `RemoteAdb` | Open and end remote ADB sessions, and run shell commands | When granted | When granted |

A person can do through the API exactly what they can do in the OhrTech Portal: every member holds
the first six, and `RemoteControl` and `RemoteAdb` are granted per member. A service account holds
only what you grant it.

## Limit a service account

Beyond permissions, a service account can be limited in three ways, set in the OhrTech Portal under
Service accounts. The limits belong to the account, so every one of its tokens has exactly the same
access.

**Devices.** Pick fleets and single devices. A fleet includes every fleet below it, including fleets
you create later. With none picked, the account reaches the whole organisation. A device outside
its scope looks like it doesn't exist (404), and the account can't move a device out of its scope.

**Networks.** CIDR ranges its tokens may be used from, such as `203.0.113.0/24`, `2001:db8::/32`
or a single address. With none, it may call from anywhere. A token used from anywhere else is
refused (401, saying why). A leaked token is then useless outside your network.

**Embed sites.** The only web origins that may show its remote control sessions, such as
`https://support.example.com`. See [embedding remote control](/management-api/guides/embed-remote-control/).

## Check what a caller can do

`GET /v1/me` returns the caller, each organisation it belongs to with its effective permissions, and
for a service account its limits:

```json
{
  "serviceAccountId": 12,
  "email": "service:help-desk",
  "globalAdmin": false,
  "globalPermissions": [],
  "clients": [{ "clientId": 3, "clientName": "Acme", "permissions": ["ViewDevices", "RemoteControl"] }],
  "serviceAccount": {
    "fleetIds": [7],
    "deviceIds": [],
    "embedOrigins": ["https://support.example.com"]
  }
}
```

`GET /v1/devices/{id}` lists, under `capabilities.yourPermissions`, what the caller may do to that
one device, so your UI can show only the buttons that will work.

## A pattern that works

- **One service account per integration** (the help desk, the inventory sync, the reporting job),
  named after it. Its tokens' last use shows in the portal, so you always know which system did what.
- **Grant the least.** A report needs `ViewDevices` only; a help desk `ViewDevices` and
  `RemoteControl`.
- **Scope it to its fleets** and **pin it to your networks** whenever it runs from known addresses.
- **Rotate tokens** by making a new one before revoking the old.
