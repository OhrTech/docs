A web app is a website your organisation gives to its devices as an app. It has its own icon on the
home screen, its own storage, and its own place in your fleets, like any app you upload. A kiosk can
run it. You don't need to change the site to make it one.

OhrTech makes the app from the site's [Web App Manifest](https://www.w3.org/TR/appmanifest/) (or
from a few fields, for a site without one), builds its APK and signs it with your organisation's
web app key. You give it to fleets as you give any app.

## Three modes

A web app runs in one of three modes. You choose the mode for each web app, and a fleet or a device
can choose a different one.

| Mode | What runs | Device SDK | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| `locked` (the default) | The live site, kept to its own pages | No | A site you don't control, or one that needs nothing from the device |
| `sdk` | The live site | Yes, on the site's own origins | Your site, when it needs the device: Wi-Fi, kiosk mode, settings, keys |
| `packaged` | The site's own files from a Signed Web Bundle, under its real origin | Yes | Your site, when each device must run a version you choose, offline too |

In every mode:

- **The app stays on the site.** It opens only the site's pages (its manifest's scope), and any
  others you allow with URL filters. Other links don't open.
- **The device's admin decides.** Camera, microphone, location, downloads, printing, new windows,
  the toolbar, client certificates and the idle reset are the web app's
  [settings](/web-apps/settings/), set per fleet or per device.
- **Notifications and push work as on the web.** The site uses the standard Notification and Push
  APIs. On an OhrTech device, push comes from your server through OhrTech push: see
  [notifications and push](/web-apps/notifications-and-push/).

## How a web app reaches devices

1. You [make the web app](/web-apps/create/) in the portal or with the Management API. OhrTech
   reads the site's manifest for the name, icon, start URL and scope.
2. OhrTech builds the app's APK, usually within a minute or two. Its package name is
   `net.ohrtech.web.w` and 20 letters and digits, derived from your organisation and the manifest's
   `id`, so the same site is always the same app.
3. You add it to fleets. Their devices install it like any of your apps.

A new name or icon in the site's manifest reaches devices only after you approve it: OhrTech checks
the manifest when you open the web app in the portal. A change to the web app's settings needs no
new version: devices get it at once.

When OhrTech improves the app's shell (the part around your site), it builds your web app again,
and devices update as they update your other apps.
