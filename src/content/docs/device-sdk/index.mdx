The Device SDK is the other half of the OhrTech SDK. Where the [Management API](/management-api/)
runs on your servers and manages many devices, the Device SDK is for **your app, on the device**: it
lets your app work with the OhrTech Agent, the system app that manages the device it runs on.

It comes as four SDKs over one Agent protocol. The Flutter, React Native and .NET ones wrap the
Kotlin library, so every call, check and error is the same whichever you use:

| For | Package | Version | Source and sample app (MIT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kotlin and Java | `net.ohrtech:device-sdk` on Maven Central | `0.3.2` | [OhrTech/device-sdk-android](https://github.com/OhrTech/device-sdk-android) |
| Flutter (Dart) | `ohrtech_device` on pub.dev | `0.1.1` | [OhrTech/device-sdk-flutter](https://github.com/OhrTech/device-sdk-flutter) |
| React Native (TypeScript, New Architecture) | `@ohrtech/device-sdk-react-native` on npm | `0.1.1` | [OhrTech/device-sdk-react-native](https://github.com/OhrTech/device-sdk-react-native) |
| .NET for Android and .NET MAUI (C#) | `OhrTech.Device` and `OhrTech.Device.Maui` on NuGet | `0.1.1` | [OhrTech/device-sdk-dotnet](https://github.com/OhrTech/device-sdk-dotnet) |

- **Runs on:** OhrTech devices, Android 13 (API 33) and later. Each SDK is for Android only.
- **Who may use it:** apps your organisation installs through OhrTech. Reading is open to every such
  app; changing the device needs the fleet's grant ([access, errors and versions](/device-sdk/access/))

Each area is an object you call directly. There is nothing to connect or close: the library binds
the Agent on the first call and keeps the binding while your app runs.

## What your app can do

| Area | What your app can do |
|---|---|
| [Wi-Fi and networks](/device-sdk/guides/wifi-and-networks/) | Read Wi-Fi state and the networks around, save, join and forget networks, turn mobile data, airplane mode and roaming on and off, and embed Android's own Wi-Fi screen in your layout |
| [Kiosk mode](/device-sdk/guides/kiosk/) | Pin your app to the screen, choose what stays available, offer staff the exit PIN, and have the Agent restart your app if it hangs |
| [Device, power and settings](/device-sdk/guides/device-power-settings/) | Read who the device is (its id, fleet and organisation), read the battery, reboot now or on a schedule, set screen timeout and brightness, read and change OhrTech device settings, install your app's own updates |
| [Managed configuration](/device-sdk/guides/managed-config/) | Read the settings your app declares, as each fleet or device sets them in the portal or the Management API |
| [Push](/device-sdk/guides/push/) | Receive messages from your own server, end to end: OhrTech carries them but can't read them |
| [Keys in the TEE](/device-sdk/guides/keys/) | Make ML-KEM, X-Wing and X25519 keys whose private halves never leave the device's TEE, and prove to your server where they are |
| [Device attestation](/device-sdk/guides/attestation/) | Prove to your server that a request comes from your unmodified app on a genuine OhrTech device, with no secret in the APK |
| [Client certificates](/device-sdk/guides/certificates/) | Get a TLS client certificate for a key made on the device, use certificates and private CAs the fleet gives your app |
| [USB devices](/device-sdk/guides/usb-devices/) | Use the USB devices and accessories the fleet approves for your app, with no "Allow access?" prompt |
| [Diagnostics](/device-sdk/guides/diagnostics/) | Send your app's own events to the device's history, and keep log lines for the log bundles support asks for |

## Which one do I need?

| | Management API | Device SDK |
|---|---|---|
| Runs | On your servers, scripts and desktop tools | Inside your app, on the device |
| Acts on | Any device in your fleets | The device it runs on |
| Talks to | `api.ohrtech.net` | The OhrTech Agent, on the device |
| Typical jobs | Help desks, inventory sync, reports, alerts, fleet-wide changes | Kiosk and single-app setups, your app's own updates, on-device settings, push and attestation for your own back end |
| Status | [Available](/management-api/) | Available |

## How the two fit together

Many products use both: the app on the device uses the Device SDK, and your back office uses the
Management API. They meet in a few places (the calls are named as in Kotlin; the other SDKs have
the same ones in their own style, such as `OhrDevice.GetInfoAsync()` in C#):

- **The same ids.** `OhrDevice.info().deviceId` is the device's `dev_…` id, and
  `OhrAccess.info().app` is your app's `app_…` id, as the Management API and the portal name them.
- **Grants.** Your organisation decides, per fleet and per app, what the app may change on the
  device: in the portal on the fleet's Applications tab, or with
  `PUT /v1/fleets/{fleetId}/apps/{appId}/sdk-access`. See [access](/device-sdk/access/).
- **Updates.** A version you upload and mark `available` with the
  [Management API](/management-api/guides/apps/) is downloaded ahead; your app installs it when it
  is ready, with `OhrUpdates.install()`.
- **Managed configuration.** You set the values your app reads with `OhrConfig` per fleet or per
  device, in the portal or with the Management API.
- **Your server.** It sends [push messages](/device-sdk/server/send-push/), checks
  [attestations](/device-sdk/server/verify-attestation/) and accepts
  [client certificates](/device-sdk/server/client-certificates/) with the Management API and its
  [.NET SDK](/management-api/sdks/dotnet/).
- **History.** Every change an app makes through the Device SDK, and every change the Agent
  refuses, appears in the device's events: in the portal, and with
  `GET /v1/devices/{deviceId}/events`.
